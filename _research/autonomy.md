Bringing **autonomy** to surgical and endoscopic robots can reduce the skill
required to perform complex procedures, improve consistency, and widen access to
high-quality care. STORM Lab develops the perception and control methods that make
this possible.

## Perception and control

Using **computer vision**, our systems interpret the live endoscopic view to
understand where they are inside the body and where to go next. Coupled with
robust **control algorithms** — including model-predictive control of magnetically
actuated devices — the robot can autonomously steer toward a target while
respecting the constraints of delicate anatomy.

## The clinician stays in command

Autonomy in our systems is **shared, not absolute**: the robot handles the
moment-to-moment navigation, but the clinician can intervene and take manual
control at any time. This human-in-the-loop design keeps safety and clinical
judgement at the centre.

## Autonomous tasks

Beyond navigation, we research autonomy for specific clinical tasks — such as
**robotic biopsy** under magnetic guidance — moving toward systems that can
reliably assist with diagnosis and treatment.
